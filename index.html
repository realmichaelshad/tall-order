    How a show poster is set

    Every line on a show poster runs the full width of the sheet. The compositor picks a face and a size for each line until it reaches both edges, so a short line comes out big and a long one gets set in a condensed face. The stack is built until it fills the sheet from top to bottom.

    The lines are locked into a forme on the bed of the press, inked with a roller, and the paper is pressed down onto them. Big wooden letters never print perfectly flat. The grain shows, the ink thins in the middle and gathers at the edges, and no two pulls come out quite the same.

    A second color means a second run through the press, which is why it never sits exactly where the first one did.
